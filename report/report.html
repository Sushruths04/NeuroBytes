NeuroBytes: EEG-to-video retrieval

Team BierBytes: Sushruth Shivaraju, Ayush Shetty · SchubKI National AI Hackathon 2026, EEG mind-reading challenge · github.com/Sushruths04/neurobytes

Task. From a 3-second, 17-channel EEG recording over the visual cortex, rank the 102 candidate test videos so that the watched video comes first.

36.03%top-1 (chance 0.98%)
67.16%top-5 (chance 4.90%)

Official development test, sub-01..04, 408 recordings. Final system: C13. Predictions: submissions/C13/.

1. Approach

  • Preprocessing: band-pass 0.5–40 Hz (Butterworth, zero-phase), 50 Hz notch, downsample to 250 Hz, per-channel z-score from each person's training recordings.
  • Video features: frozen pretrained models embed the candidate videos (AlexNet, ResNet-50, CLIP, DINOv2, SigLIP, VideoMAE, V-JEPA 2, S3D, CLAP, Whisper and others; exact versions in MODELS.md).
  • Scorers: ridge regressions map windowed EEG to video features (PCA 150/256, penalty by 5-fold CV). For each person, other people's EEG is aligned into that person's channel space using the 695 videos everyone watched, then pooled.
  • Networks: per-person 17→32 spatial layer, 3-layer temporal CNN, one head per video embedding; symmetric InfoNCE, AdamW, 40 epochs, 5 seeds; 6 and 10 heads.
  • Fusion and balancing: scores are fused by reciprocal rank with hub correction. CSLS removes "hub" videos, and Sinkhorn makes each video win about once across a person's 102 recordings.
score = 0.85 × C10 blend + 0.075 × network h6 + 0.075 × network h10 → CSLS (k=2) → Sinkhorn (3 iter., τ=0.5) C10 blend = 0.85 × (509/586 × winfuse + 77/586 × ours77) + 0.15 × T2

All settings were chosen by cross-validation on held-out training videos. Python 3.11, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, PyTorch 2.4. Weights: release v1.0. Docker: ghcr.io/sushruths04/neurobytes.

3. Failure cases

  • Near misses dominate. About half of the misses still have the correct video in the top 5. The model usually finds the right neighbourhood, and the noise in a single recording decides the last step.
  • Misses are noise, not hard videos. Per-video difficulty barely correlates between people (r = 0.16), so no fixed set of videos fails for everyone.
  • One participant is harder. sub-03 reaches 26.5% / 57.8%, against 32–49% / 69–74% for the others, because of lower signal quality. Four targeted fixes all lowered accuracy, so none was kept.
  • Late signal is unusable. Decoding from any window after about 800 ms is at chance, even when locked to the frame on screen.
  • Approaches that did not help: hard one-to-one assignment (Hungarian) instead of Sinkhorn, banded ridge, pooling other people's EEG without alignment (−7.05 points), larger seed and window ensembles, per-person network fine-tuning, and caption features.

4. Lessons learned

  • When to look matters more than model size. Restricting the EEG to 50–400 ms was the largest single gain (winfuse top-5 39.95% → 47.30%). Mid-level CNN layers (ResNet-50 layer 3, AlexNet fc6) match this window better than the outputs of larger models.
  • Use the structure of the task. Each video is watched once, so balancing the score matrix with Sinkhorn added 6.13 points of top-5 without touching the EEG.
  • Correct for hubs and fuse ranks. Rank fusion lets hundreds of scorers be combined without calibration. CSLS added 4.4 points of top-1.
  • Learn across people only after aligning them. Every head mixes the brain's signals differently. Aligned pooling helps every participant; raw pooling hurts.
  • Many simple models beat one complex one. About 590 closed-form ridge scorers and two small CNNs, chosen on validation, gave a stable result that rebuilds bit-identically.
